# Five-person comprehension check

Recruit five adults who have no prior knowledge of El Niño. Include mobile users and people with different levels of confidence reading weather information. Run individually; do not explain the site before the tasks. Obtain consent, keep notes anonymous and collect no health or financial information.

Give each person the dashboard and two minutes to explore. Ask:

1. In your own words, what is happening now? Where did that information come from, and when was it issued?
2. What could happen next? Does the largest percentage mean certainty? Does it tell you the chance of rain in your town?
3. What does this mean for India this season? What would you check before changing a farming, water or outdoor-work plan?
4. Switch to Global and choose a region. Are the explanation and source a local forecast? Share that view and explain what the recipient will see.
5. Open the ocean history. Is it a forecast, and does a higher observed value automatically change the official phase?

Record each answer, any confusing wording, time to find the source and whether help was required. Ask one final question: “What one sentence would make this clearer?”

For each item mark understood, partly understood or misunderstood, and quote the relevant words. Do not equate clicking the right button with comprehension. Aim for all five to distinguish present diagnosis, future chance and local weather. Revise any wording that creates false certainty, then repeat affected tasks with new participants. This document is a protocol, not evidence that testing has taken place.
