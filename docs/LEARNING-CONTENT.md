# Understand page: learning design and maintenance

The page offers a quick introduction, deeper reading and a video path. Visitors can jump between sections, compare the three phases using the existing illustration, read a clearly marked 70/20/10 probability example, connect the topic to India's two monsoons, ask common questions, try a formative three-question check and explore a glossary and external resources. No multilingual interface is included.

The quiz is local to the page. It gives explanatory feedback for every answer, allows another choice and can restart. It does not send answers, create accounts, add tracking or certify comprehension. The 100-dot example is decorative with a complete text equivalent and explicitly does not represent the current forecast. Current outlooks remain on the dashboard.

## Source mapping

- NOAA introduction: https://www.noaa.gov/understanding-el-nino — phase definitions, differing regional effects and the role of the atmosphere.
- NOAA PMEL: https://www.pmel.noaa.gov/elnino/what-is-el-nino/ and /what-is-la-nina/ — trade winds, warm water movement and cooler-water upwelling.
- NOAA CPC: https://www.cpc.ncep.noaa.gov/products/analysis_monitoring/enso/roni/announcement.php — the observed relative index and the distinction from diagnosis; official probabilities page for reading chances.
- IMD: https://mausam.imd.gov.in/imd_latest/monsoonfaq.pdf — seasonal rainfall, averages and multiple monsoon influences.
- MAUSAM research: https://mausamjournal.imd.gov.in/index.php/MAUSAM/article/view/1527 — historical northeast monsoon associations in southeastern peninsular India. This tendency is not presented as a forecast for the current year or the whole country.
- NASA: https://science.nasa.gov/earth/explore/el-nino/ and https://science.nasa.gov/climate-change/faq/whats-the-difference-between-weather-and-climate/ — satellite visuals, the natural cycle within a changing climate, and weather versus climate.
- NOAA JetStream: https://www.noaa.gov/jetstream — additional weather lessons.

Links open the original publisher in a new tab; there are no autoplaying players, video downloads, unverified YouTube IDs or third-party embeds. Card art is original SVG decoration rather than a reproduced video thumbnail. Questions beside each link give the visitor something to notice while watching.

## Review and next improvements

Check linked resources quarterly and after provider URL changes. Keep tutorials separate from dated forecast data; never turn the example percentages into a live status display. The browser checks cover quiz feedback/reset, resource counts, external-embed absence and 320/390px layouts. Human accessibility and comprehension reviews remain necessary.

Use docs/COMPREHENSION-TEST.md with real visitors. Ask them to explain why phase chances differ from local rainfall chances and identify the season relevant to their location. The highest-value next steps are revisions based on those answers and a teacher-tested activity sheet; avoid adding more controls before knowing where learners get confused.
